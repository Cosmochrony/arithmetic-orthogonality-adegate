  \begin{abstract}
    The generation-split value $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ is often presented as a derived prediction of the spectral
    stratigraphy of the corpus.
    This note isolates its exact logical status.
    First, $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ is a theorem modulo the ADE case-selection gate: once a case is selected whose
    class-complete spectrum carries the external level ratio $3:2$, the generation-deficit normal form forces
    $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ and $\kappa=\tfrac{5}{12}$ algebraically, with no further input.
    Second, the first-principles status of $\epsilon$ is therefore exactly the first-principles status of the gate,
    which this note does not settle.
    We then examine a candidate arithmetic obstruction.
    In the compositum $K_q=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q,\sqrt{5})$ the Born--Infeld parity $c\mapsto q-c$, the restriction of
    complex conjugation $\zeta_q\mapsto\zeta_q^{-1}$ (the candidate fibre involution of the foundations, taken here as a
    premise), fixes $\sqrt{5}$, whereas the Galois action
    $\sigma:\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$, which exchanges the two two-dimensional irreducible characters of the binary
    icosahedral group $2I\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$, lies in the other direct factor of the Galois group: the two are
    arithmetically orthogonal.
    A character-table audit of $2I$, computed from the group, proves that inner conjugations, the central element
    $-I$ and the projective involution $J_\Pi$ induce the identity permutation of $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$, whereas
    $\sigma$ induces the permutation $(2\,2')(3\,3')$; under [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of} the central reflection
    $R_b$ acts as $-I$ does.
    The remaining generators of the spin stratum enter as stated clauses of different status: the Schur-projection and
    tensor-functor clauses are modelling assertions, and the A4-locking clause holds under the A4 hypotheses
    ([H-Gen], $J_\Pi$-covariance, a locked pair) and is also a modelling assertion.
    The completeness of this generator list is a modelling premise, not a proved fact.
    Modulo these clauses and that premise, no operation of the spin stratum realises $\sigma$.
    This is a statement about $\sigma$ and not about the ADE gate: the outer automorphism fixes the order-four class
    and maps each of the order-five and order-ten loci to itself, so a link between the gate and $\sigma$ is not
    established here, and no conclusion on the gate is drawn.
    The extension to a recursive tower of admissible fibre operations is conditional on the same modelling premise [M]
    (completeness of the list, which implies that every admissible operation is carrier-preserving) and, for the
    central reflection, on [H-WS]: recursive non-injectivity then multiplies the vertical parity type and cannot supply
    $\sigma$.
    The Lorentzian chiral lift ($S_L\oplus S_R$, $\mathfrak{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$)
    is an input that this note does not construct; its status is that of the spin-solder hypothesis [H-Spin] of
    Q14~\cite{Beau2026q14}, which no source supplies.
        Under [H-Spin], [H-WS], the modelling premise, the inherited central-phase convention of the chiral orientation
    note~\cite{Beau2026cho} and a further explicit premise [B] (the lift supplies, beyond the spin-stratum operations,
    only the central character and the complexification unit $\mathrm{i}$ acting by complex conjugation; this is
    asserted, not derived, and the Galois group of $K_q(\mathrm{i})$ does contain elements moving $\sqrt{5}$),
    the lift induces no action on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, so that the residual spin-Galois
    caveat of that note does not arise within the present spin stratum; the overlap $\rho_\chi=1$ holds there by
    definition of $\sigma_L$, and the conclusion $N_A\neq0$ is conditional on all of these, in particular on [B].
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}

    The generation sector of the corpus produces a three-level deficit structure whose outer ratio matches the
    external ADE ratio $3:2$ of a selected case, and this matching fixes the split value $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ and
    the common scale $\kappa=\tfrac{5}{12}$~\cite{Beau2026q14,Beau2026a4,Beau2026bim}.
    The value $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ has occasionally been described as derived.
    This note draws the line precisely, because the distinction between a posited value and a value forced once a case
    is selected is the whole content of the matter.

    The clarification is in two parts.
    The first part is a trivial decomposition that nonetheless settles the status question: the algebra that takes the
    $3:2$ ratio to $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ is general and case-independent, so all of the first-principles content lives
    in the upstream selection of the case~(Section~\ref{sec:conditional}).
    The second part examines a candidate arithmetic obstruction and states exactly what is and is not proved about it.
    The Galois action $\sigma:\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$ exchanges the two two-dimensional irreducible characters of the
    binary icosahedral group $2I$.
        The foundations of~\cite{Beau2026m} state the Born--Infeld parity $c\leftrightarrow q-c$, which acts as complex
    conjugation on the central character and fixes the real quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, as the candidate
    fibre involution of the programme (a structurally motivated hypothesis there, the fibre identification being an
    open classification problem); this note takes it as a premise.
    That the lift supplies no other Galois-type action is not a result of the foundations but a further premise of this
    note (Section~\ref{sec:chiral}, premise [B]).
    These two actions sit in distinct direct factors of the Galois group of the natural compositum, so the parity is not
    a partial version of $\sigma$; it is orthogonal to it~(Section~\ref{sec:organizing}).
    A character-table audit then shows that the operations listed for the spin stratum do not realise
    $\sigma$~(Sections~\ref{sec:negative}--\ref{sec:rigidity}).
    This is a statement about $\sigma$.
    That the ADE gate requires $\sigma$ is not established: the outer automorphism fixes the order-four class and
    preserves each of the order-five and order-ten loci (Remark~\ref{rem:gate-link}), so no conclusion on the gate is
    drawn.

    The recursive non-injectivity theorem of ENI~\cite{Beau2026l} guarantees that each stratum of a tower of genuinely
    distinct emergent levels carries its own non-injectivity, but it constrains existence, not arithmetic type.
        For the base fibre the involution is the candidate Born--Infeld parity, a hypothesis of~\cite{Beau2026m} taken here
    as a premise and not derived (Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}); what is proved about it is only that, as complex
    conjugation, it fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:organizing}).
    For the spin stratum, a bounded character-table audit of $2I\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$ shows that the inner
    conjugations, $-I$ and $J_\Pi$ induce the identity permutation of $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$ (the central reflection $R_b$
    under [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}; the Schur-projection, tensor-functor and A4-locking clauses under the
    modelling assertions and A4 hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}), so that none of the listed
    operations realises $\sigma$~(Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}, Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
    Whether every conceivable recursive stratum inherits the parity type is treated in Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}
    relative to a modelling premise [M]: the admissible fibre operations are exactly the compositions of a listed
    set, each carrier-preserving.
    Since $\sigma$ is not carrier-preserving, a finite recursive tower of such operations cannot generate it, and
    preserves the arithmetic type; the premise [M] itself is not proved.
    The chiral orientation note~\cite{Beau2026cho} proves the orientation chain
    $[\mathrm{H\text{-}orient}]$ within the inherited Heisenberg central-phase convention and under [H-WS]
    of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, and isolates a single residual caveat: a possible additional spin-Galois phase of the
    full Lorentzian chiral lift, orthogonal to $\zeta_q$, in particular a $\sqrt{5}$ or ADE-spin factor.
    Section~\ref{sec:chiral} examines that caveat.
    It takes the Lorentzian chiral lift as an input that this note does not construct (the spin-solder hypothesis
    [H-Spin] of~\cite{Beau2026q14}, supplied by no source), and shows that under [H-Spin], [H-WS], [M], the
    inherited central-phase convention and the explicit premise [B] (the lift supplies only the central character and
    the complexification unit, by complex conjugation) the lift induces no action on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$; the chiral weight
    $\sigma_L$ is the Weyl conjugation type, which lives in the parity factor and not in the $\sqrt{5}$ factor.
    The conclusion is conditional on all of these inputs, and $\rho_\chi=1$ holds there by definition of $\sigma_L$.

    Section~\ref{sec:status} records the epistemic stratification and the downstream consequence: observable masses
    route through the explicit operator $E_\Pi$ and the Yukawa sector regardless of how the gate is
    resolved~\cite{Beau2026fmnote,Beau2026prs}.

  \section{The conditional split}
    \label{sec:conditional}

    We work with the generation-deficit normal form of the dynamical spectrum on the generation
    factor~\cite{Beau2026q14}.
    Let $s_0$ be the central eigenvalue, fixed by the projective involution $J_\Pi$, and let $s_\pm$ be the two outer
    eigenvalues, in the normalisation $s_0=1$.

    \begin{definition}[Exit deficit]
For an eigenvalue $s$ with central value $s_0$, the exit deficit is $d_{\mathrm{exit}}(s):=s_0-s$.
The normal form $s_0=1,\ s_\pm=\tfrac{1}{2}\pm\epsilon$ gives
$d_{\mathrm{exit}}(s_0)=0$ and $d_{\mathrm{exit}}(s_\pm)=\tfrac{1}{2}\mp\epsilon$.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{lemma}[Algebraic ratio lemma]
      \label{lem:algebraic}
      If the outer exit deficits are in the ratio $3:2$, that is
      \[
        \frac{d_{\mathrm{exit}}(s_-)}{d_{\mathrm{exit}}(s_+)}=\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}+\epsilon}{\tfrac{1}{2}-\epsilon}=\frac{3}{2},
      \]
      then $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$.
      If in addition a single common scale $\kappa$ satisfies $\kappa\, d_{\mathrm{exit}}(s_\pm)=|\lambda-1|$ on the two
      outer levels with $\{|\lambda_1-1|,|\lambda_3-1|\}=\{\tfrac{1}{6},\tfrac{1}{4}\}$, then $\kappa=\tfrac{5}{12}$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      From $2(\tfrac{1}{2}+\epsilon)=3(\tfrac{1}{2}-\epsilon)$ one obtains $1+2\epsilon=\tfrac{3}{2}-3\epsilon$, hence
      $5\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{2}$ and $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$.
      The outer deficits are then $\{\tfrac{2}{5},\tfrac{3}{5}\}$, and
      $\kappa=(\tfrac{1}{4})/(\tfrac{3}{5})=(\tfrac{1}{6})/(\tfrac{2}{5})=\tfrac{5}{12}$, consistent on both levels.
    \end{proof}

    The lemma is trivial and entirely case-independent: it uses only the ratio $3:2$, not any property of the binary
    icosahedral group or of the generating set.
    The status of $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ is therefore the status of the hypothesis that supplies the ratio $3:2$.

    \begin{theorem}[Conditional ADE split]
      \label{thm:conditional}
      Assume the ADE case-selection gate selects the central-lift class over the $\sqrt{5}$-locus, that is the parity
      class over the $A_5$ five-cycle locus, whose class-complete Cayley spectrum has external ratio
      \[
        \frac{|\lambda_3-1|}{|\lambda_1-1|}=\frac{3}{2}
      \]
      with $\{|\lambda_1-1|,|\lambda_3-1|\}=\{\tfrac{1}{6},\tfrac{1}{4}\}$ and central level
      $\lambda_2=1$~\cite{Beau2026a6,Beau2026a4}.
      Then the generation-deficit normal form forces, by Lemma~\ref{lem:algebraic},
      \[
        \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}+\epsilon}{\tfrac{1}{2}-\epsilon}=\frac{3}{2},\qquad
        \epsilon=\frac{1}{10},\qquad \kappa=\frac{5}{12}.
      \]
      The split value is not an independent parameter once the ADE case is selected.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{remark}[Status]
      \label{rem:status}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional} is conditional, not predictive.
Its single nontrivial hypothesis is the gate.
The first-principles status of $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ is exactly the first-principles status of the gate, and
nothing in the theorem bears on why the $\sqrt{5}$-locus is selected.
The wording avoids committing to ``order five'' versus ``order ten'' for the generating class: the variable-valence
exit analysis uses the order-five generating set~\cite{Beau2026a6}, while the bounded-flux admissibility analysis
uses the order-ten classes $10a\cup 10b$~\cite{Beau2026a1}, and the two are identified by the central lift, as made
precise in Corollary~\ref{cor:tiebreak}.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The arithmetic organizing lemma}
    \label{sec:organizing}

    Two arithmetic data enter the construction from independent parts of the apparatus.
    The root of unity $\zeta_q=e^{2\pi i/q}$ is the central character of the fibre group $\Heis$, fixed by the
    Stone--von Neumann identification of the Weil representation $V_\rho$, a modelling dictionary of the foundations
    rather than a consequence of their axioms~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    The quadratic surd $\sqrt{5}$ is the character field of the two two-dimensional irreducible representations of the
    binary icosahedral group $2I$, where it appears through the golden ratio identity
    $\chi(10a)+\chi(10b)=\varphi-\bar\varphi=1$ on the order-ten classes, with $\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2$ and
    $\bar\varphi=\varphi-1=1/\varphi$ (the latter is not the Galois conjugate $-1/\varphi$ of $\varphi$)~\cite{Beau2026a1}.
    These origins are independent: the first is a property of the fibre group on which the axioms act, the second is a
    representation-theoretic property of the spin-stratum subgroup.

    \begin{lemma}[Arithmetic orthogonality]
      \label{lem:organizing}
      Let $q$ be prime with $q\neq 5$, and let $K_q=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q,\sqrt{5})$.
      Then $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ are linearly disjoint over $\mathbb{Q}$, so
      \[
        \Gal(K_q/\mathbb{Q})\;\cong\;\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)/\mathbb{Q})\times\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})/\mathbb{Q}).
      \]
      The Born--Infeld parity $c\mapsto q-c$ is the restriction of complex conjugation; as an element of
      $\Gal(K_q/\mathbb{Q})$ it is
      \[
        \text{BI parity}=(\zeta_q\mapsto\zeta_q^{-1},\ \sqrt{5}\mapsto\sqrt{5}).
      \]
      The Galois action separating the two two-dimensional irreducible characters of $2I$ is
      \[
        \text{Galois-spin}=(\zeta_q\mapsto\zeta_q,\ \sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}).
      \]
      These lie in distinct direct factors.
      In particular the Born--Infeld parity fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ pointwise and is not the Galois-spin action.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      The unique quadratic subfield of $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)$ is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^{*}})$ with
      $q^{*}=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}q$.
      For prime $q\neq 5$ one has $q^{*}\neq 5$ up to squares, so $\sqrt{5}\notin\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)$ and
      $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)\cap\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})=\mathbb{Q}$.
      Linear disjointness gives the direct-product decomposition of the Galois group.
      The central character satisfies
      $e^{2\pi i c/q}\mapsto e^{2\pi i(q-c)/q}=e^{-2\pi i c/q}=\overline{e^{2\pi i c/q}}$ under $c\mapsto q-c$, so the
      Born--Infeld parity is complex conjugation on the $\zeta_q$ factor; since $\sqrt{5}$ is real, complex conjugation
      fixes it.
      The Galois-spin action is the nontrivial element of $\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})/\mathbb{Q})$ and fixes $\zeta_q$.
      The two thus occupy distinct direct factors.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Why the disjunction is not accidental]
The direct-factor separation reflects the independence of the two origins.
The direct-factor statement itself is pure Galois theory and does not depend on the axioms.
Relative to [M] (Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}) the listed operations act on the fibre $\Heis$ and on its cyclotomic
factor $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)$ and have no action on the factor in which $\sqrt{5}$ lives; that no further
operation does so is the content of [M], not a consequence of the lemma.
The orthogonality of the two factors is structural, not numerical.
    \end{remark}

  \section{The central-lift tie-break}
    \label{sec:tiebreak}

    The order-ten elements of $2I$ are the order-five elements multiplied by the central element $-I$, since for an
    order-five $g$ one has $(-Ig)^{5}=-I$ and $(-Ig)^{10}=I$.
    The Weil lift of $-I$ is the central reflection $R_b=W(-I)=\mathrm{FT}^{2}$.
    That it acts on the chiral carrier as the scalar $-1$, so that $[\gamma_5,R_b]=0$ and $R_b$ realises multiplication
    by $-I$, is the hypothesis [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, supplied by no source; the stronger group-level formulation (a homomorphism from the
    finite Weil group to $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ taking $W(-I)$ to $-I$) is not available at the corpus primes.
    (On $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_q)$ itself $\mathrm{FT}^2$ is the reflection $f(x)\mapsto f(-x)$, not a scalar: [H-WS] is a
    stipulation about the chiral carrier.)
    Every use below of $R_b$ as the central element $-I$ of $2I$ is under [H-WS].

    \begin{corollary}[Order-five versus order-ten is below parity resolution]
      \label{cor:tiebreak}
      The central lift $\times(-I)$ is realised by $R_b=W(-I)$, under [H-WS]; $R_b$ is unitary and distinct from the
      antiunitary Born--Infeld parity $J_\Pi$ ($\varphi=R_b\circ J_\Pi$ in~\cite{Beau2026q11of}).
      Modulo the central lift, the projective quotient that the construction realises identifies the order-five and order-ten classes rather than distinguishing them.
            Relative to the modelling premise [M], the order-five versus order-ten alternative is not selectable by the
      listed operations; it is a central-lift ambiguity that the parity does not resolve.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      The pair of classes is exchanged by $\times(-I)$, whose realisation is $R_b$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:organizing} the parity acts only on the $\zeta_q$ factor and fixes $\sqrt{5}$, and so does $R_b$
      under [H-WS]; together they permute the classes by the central lift while leaving the Galois data invariant.
      Quotienting by the central lift identifies the two classes; it does not order them.
            Hence the listed operations (the parity and, under [H-WS], $R_b$) supply no selector between order five and order
      ten; that the framework supplies none beyond them is not proved, and rests on [M].
      With the labelling convention $10a:=-(5a)$, $10b:=-(5b)$ (so that $\chi_2(5a)=\bar\varphi$, Appendix~\ref{app:audit}),
      the central lift $\times(-I)$ exchanges the classes $5a\leftrightarrow10a$ and $5b\leftrightarrow10b$, whereas $\sigma$
      preserves the order-five and the order-ten loci and exchanges only $5a\leftrightarrow5b$ and $10a\leftrightarrow10b$
      (\texttt{code/outer\_automorphism\_classes.py}): the two are different maps.
    \end{proof}

    The parity fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ and permutes no class of $2I$ (every character is real); the central lift
    $\times(-I)$ is the separate operation $R_b$ (under [H-WS]).
    The listed operations therefore neither separate order five from order ten, nor separate the two classes $5a$ and $5b$, which
    $\sigma$ exchanges.

  \section{The negative theorem}
    \label{sec:negative}

    \begin{theorem}[The Galois action $\sigma$ is not realised by the listed spin-stratum operations]
      \label{thm:negative}
      Let $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}}$ be the group generated by the operations of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}.
      Under the clauses and hypotheses stated there ([H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of} for $R_b$; the A4 hypotheses for the
      locking; the modelling assertions for the tensor functors and the Schur projection), every element of
      $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}}$ acts trivially on $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$ and fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ pointwise; in
      particular no element induces the permutation $(2\,2')(3\,3')$ of $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$ induced by $\sigma$.
      By Lemma~\ref{lem:organizing} the field automorphisms so generated lie in the factor
      $\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)/\mathbb{Q})$ of $\Gal(K_q/\mathbb{Q})$.
      Consequently $\sigma$, the nontrivial element of $\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})/\mathbb{Q})$, is not supplied by these
      operations; modulo the modelling premise [M] of Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}, they are all the operations that
      A1--A4, the recursive non-injectivity of ENI~Corollary~6~\cite{Beau2026l} and the A4 locking make available at
      the spin stratum.
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      By Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity} the listed operations each fix $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, so by
      Lemma~\ref{lem:organizing} their generated group lies in $\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)/\mathbb{Q})$ when the operations
      are modelled as automorphisms of $K_q$.
      The $\sqrt{5}$-fixing automorphisms form a subgroup, closed under composition and inverse, and $\sigma$ is not in it.
      Equivalently, the generated group acts trivially on $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$, whereas $\sigma$ acts as $(2\,2')(3\,3')$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[What is not shown: the link between the gate and $\sigma$]
      \label{rem:gate-link}
      Theorem~\ref{thm:negative} is a statement about $\sigma$.
      It does not say that the ADE case-selection gate requires $\sigma$.
      The gate selects the central-lift class over the $\sqrt{5}$-locus, that is the order-five and order-ten classes
      rather than the neutral order-four class.
      An exact enumeration over $\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$ (\texttt{code/outer\_automorphism\_classes.py}) shows that the outer
      automorphism fixes the order-four class and maps each of the loci $5a\cup5b$ and $10a\cup10b$ onto itself,
      exchanging $5a\leftrightarrow5b$ and $10a\leftrightarrow10b$.
      Both candidate loci are therefore $\sigma$-invariant: $\sigma$ would be needed to tell $5a$ from $5b$, or $2$
      from $2'$, not to choose between the order-four and the order-five locus.
      Both two-dimensional characters moreover vanish on the order-four class.
      A link between the gate and $\sigma$ would need a separate argument that the selection of the locus is carried by
      an action on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$; none is given here.
      This note therefore draws no conclusion on the gate: its first-principles status stays that of
      Remark~\ref{rem:status}.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Scope]
      Theorem~\ref{thm:negative} concerns the listed operations, with the clauses of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity} carrying their own status ($R_b$ under [H-WS]; the A4-locking clause under the
      A4 hypotheses; the tensor-functor and Schur-projection clauses as modelling assertions).
      The extension to every conceivable recursive stratum, Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity}, is relative to the modelling
      premise [M].
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[Independent computational evidence on the loci]
            Separately from Theorem~\ref{thm:negative}, the bounded-flux admissibility note reports the order-ten classes
      $10a\cup10b$ as the generating set of $2I$ in its analysis and lists $\chi_2=0$ on the order-four class in its
      character table~\cite{Beau2026a1} (its neutral generators are the order-four generators of the quaternion group
      $Q_8$; calling the order-four class of $2I$ the neutral convention, as in Section~\ref{sec:status}, is this
      note's own reading), and the penalised spectral-capacity note
      finds, at equal valence $d=24$, the binary icosahedral group $2I$ (generating set $C_3\cup C_4$) dominating
      $A_5$ (all five-cycles) for every $\alpha>0$ (its Table~\texttt{d24})~\cite{Beau2026a2}.
      That comparison is between groups at fixed valence, not between the order-four and the order-five locus inside
      $2I$.
      This is a different argument, not a consequence of Theorem~\ref{thm:negative} and not re-derived here.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Type-rigidity along the tower}
    \label{sec:rigidity}

    \paragraph{Modelling premise [M].}
    The statements of this section concern the operations of a list.
    The premise [M] is that the operations available at a spin stratum, at every level of the tower, are exactly the
    compositions of those listed in Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}.
    This completeness of the generator list is a modelling premise, not a proved fact, and every statement labelled
    ``relative to [M]'' below depends on it.
    Each listed operation is carrier-preserving (clauses (a)--(d)), so [M] implies that every admissible fibre
    operation is carrier-preserving; the carrier-level Lemma~\ref{lem:no-horizontal-admissible-operation} uses only
    this weaker consequence, whereas the closing clause of Theorem~\ref{thm:negative} (``all the operations that
    \dots make available'') uses completeness itself.
    Throughout, ``relative to [M]'' includes the statuses of clauses (a)--(d) of
    Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}, in particular [H-WS] for $R_b$ and the A4 hypotheses, together with the
    candidate base-fibre parity taken as a premise.

    \begin{proposition}[Spin-stratum type-rigidity for the listed operations]
      \label{prop:spinrigidity}
      Let $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}}$ be the group generated by the operations available at the spin stratum of the
      present corpus construction: inner conjugations in $2I$, the central element $-I$, the central reflection
      $R_b=W(-I)$, the projective involution $J_\Pi$, the tensor functors of $\mathrm{Rep}(2I)$, the Schur projection,
      and the A4 locking.
      The clauses have different status.
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item[(a)] \emph{Proved by the character-table audit} (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
        Inner conjugations, $-I$ and $J_\Pi$ induce the identity permutation of $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$.
        The outer automorphism of $2I\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$, which realises $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$, induces
        $(2\,2')(3\,3')$ on $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$ and exchanges the classes $5a\leftrightarrow5b$, $10a\leftrightarrow10b$,
        fixing the others.
        \item[(b)] \emph{Under [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}.} $R_b=W(-I)$ acts on the chiral carrier as the scalar $-1$,
        as $-I$ does, and induces the identity permutation.
        \item[(c)] \emph{Under the A4 hypotheses, and a modelling assertion.} Where a locked pair $\pm s_*$ exists (a hypothesis of the A4 note), under
        [H-Gen] of~\cite{Beau2026prs}, the $J_\Pi$-covariance and the diagonal form of the $J_\Pi$-odd lemma
        of~\cite{Beau2026a4} (and [H-F], [H-Nd] for the orientation branch), the locking contributes the sign of $u$,
        odd under the parity of the $J_\Pi$-odd modulus, in the parity factor.
        \item[(d)] \emph{Modelling assertion, not part of the audit.} The tensor functors (integer fusion coefficients)
        and the Schur projection (defined through $J_\Pi$-parity and symplectic data) fix $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.
      \end{enumerate}
      Consequently, modulo clauses (b)--(d), $\langle\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}}\rangle$ acts trivially on
      $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$ and fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ pointwise, so it contains no element inducing
      $(2\,2')(3\,3')$, that is no realisation of $\sigma$; relative to [M] this covers every operation of the spin
      stratum.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Clause (a) is the character-table audit of $2I\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$, carried out from the group in
      Appendix~\ref{app:audit} and validated by the orthogonality relations.
      Inner conjugations act trivially on $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$, since characters are class functions.
      The central element $-I$ acts on each irreducible representation by the scalar
      $\chi(-I)/\chi(1)=\pm1$, a spinorial grading that permutes nothing.
      The projective involution $J_\Pi$ is complex conjugation on the central character; since every character of $2I$
      is real-valued, it fixes every irreducible representation.
      The audit does not cover the other generators.
      Clause (b) transfers the action of $-I$ to $R_b$ by the scalar $-1$ of [H-WS].
      For clause (c), the sign of $u$ is odd under the parity $s\mapsto-s$, $u(-s)=-u(s)$, under the stated hypotheses
      of~\cite{Beau2026bim,Beau2026a4}; this is the $J_\Pi$-odd chiral $\mathbb{Z}/2$, in the parity factor, not the
      Galois action $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$, and the magnitude $|u|$ is not predicted there, being dictionary-bound
      through $\mathcal{N}_A$; that the locking contributes nothing beyond this sign is part of the modelling, so that clause (c) is under the A4
      hypotheses and also a modelling assertion.
      Clause (d) is asserted, not computed.
      Since the $\sqrt{5}$-fixing automorphisms form a subgroup, closed under composition and inverse, no iteration,
      central lift, projection or A4 locking built from these generators can induce the permutation $(2\,2')(3\,3')$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{lemma}[Full recursive type-rigidity, relative to the modelling premise {[M]}]
      \label{lem:rigidity}
      Every non-injective projection of the tower (existence: ENI~Corollary~6), including the recursive non-injectivities at
      every stratum above the base fibre, acts on the cyclotomic factor $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)$ and fixes the real
      quadratic factor $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, relative to [M].
    \end{lemma}

    Three regimes of this statement have different status, and conflating them is exactly the error to avoid.

        For the base fibre $\Pi_1$ the involution is taken as a premise (status row below: ``candidate''), not derived in
    this note and not proved in the foundations.
    The foundations state the conjugate pair $c\leftrightarrow q-c$, acting by complex conjugation, as the candidate
    parity structure of the programme: reading it as the minimal admissible fibre is a structurally motivated
    hypothesis, the fibre identification being an open classification problem~\cite{Beau2026m}, and identifying the
    fibre with the Heisenberg carrier $V_\rho$ is a modelling dictionary~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    The parity-orbit companion shows moreover that evenness of the Born--Infeld action does not force
    $\{\chi,-\chi\}$ into the fibres~\cite{Beau2026a22}.
    Granting the candidate, Lemma~\ref{lem:organizing} shows that this involution lies in
    $\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)/\mathbb{Q})$ and fixes $\sqrt{5}$; that part is proved, the candidate is not.

    For the spin stratum the statement holds for the listed operations, with the status of each clause given in
    Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}.

    For the abstract claim over every conceivable recursive stratum the statement is not an arithmetic fact but is
    relative to [M].
    ENI~Corollary~6 forces $S_{\Pi_i}>0$ at each stratum of a tower of genuinely distinct emergent levels, but it
    constrains existence of the non-injectivity, not its arithmetic type~\cite{Beau2026l,Beau2026ninote}.
    Proposition~\ref{prop:verticality-admissible-identifications} derives the statement from [M]: ENI supplies no
    operation in the $\sqrt{5}$ factor, every admissible identification being carrier-preserving and hence vertical.

    \begin{remark}[Reduction to a type-preservation question]
      \label{rem:type-preservation}
The full-tower rigidity problem reduces to a type-preservation question.
Recursive non-injectivity alone does not distinguish the vertical parity type from the horizontal Galois type.
The entropy increase supplied by recursive emergence records the multiplicity of fibre identifications, not their
arithmetic type, so the monotonicity $S_{\Pi_2\circ\Pi_1}\geq S_{\Pi_1}$ of ENI~Corollary~6 cannot by itself decide
the type.
The relevant invariant is therefore not the fibre entropy itself, but the Galois-module type of the fibre
identification.

For the binary icosahedral stratum the vertical operations, generated by central parity, Schur scalars, internal
conjugation, and the already fixed admissibility involutions, act inside each irreducible carrier and fix the
quadratic field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}.
By contrast the operation exchanging the two spinorial two-dimensional icosahedral types is the horizontal
Galois involution $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$, the outer automorphism of $2I\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$ that permutes the
two two-dimensional irreducible characters.
Since the vertical operations form a subgroup, a finite tower of vertical identifications preserves the arithmetic
type: recursive emergence multiplies the parity type already present without manufacturing the Galois transport,
\[
  \text{recursive non-injectivity}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \begin{cases}
    \text{vertical fibre multiplication, preserving }\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}),\ \text{or}\\
    \text{horizontal Galois transport, exchanging }2\leftrightarrow 2'.
  \end{cases}
\]
The remaining rigidity question is whether every admissible recursive stratum is forced to be vertical, or whether
an admissible stratum can carry the horizontal automorphism exchanging the two two-dimensional icosahedral types.
This is the type-inheritance content of Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity}, distinct from entropy preservation, and it is
reduced below to the premise [M].
Theorem~\ref{thm:negative} does not depend on it, except for its completeness clause.
    \end{remark}

    A possible ambiguity must be addressed before the question can be treated.
    The quadratic surd $\sqrt{5}$ already occurs in the icosahedral geometry, through the golden-ratio Gram data of the
    binary-icosahedral embedding, so the admissibility data may well contain invariants defined over
    $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.
    This by itself provides no horizontal operation.
    What must be excluded is not the occurrence of $\sqrt{5}$, but the existence of an admissible operation
    implementing its non-trivial Galois involution $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$.

    \begin{lemma}[No horizontal admissible operation, relative to {[M]}]
      \label{lem:no-horizontal-admissible-operation}
      The admissibility data A1--A4 and the Born--Infeld parity may use the binary-icosahedral Gram geometry, hence
      may contain invariants defined over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, but, relative to [M], they supply no admissible
      operation acting by the non-trivial automorphism of this field.
      Equivalently, they contain no operation in the external coset that exchanges the two inequivalent
      two-dimensional binary-icosahedral types.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{proof}
      By [M] an admissible fibre operation acts inside the admissible carrier: it identifies states, phases, central
      characters, or parity-related vectors within the realised representation, so it preserves the carrier as a
      subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and commutes with the $2I$-action up to the inner and central data.
      By Schur's lemma such an operation preserves each isotypic component, hence fixes the isomorphism class of every
      irreducible carrier and fixes the character field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ pointwise.
      The involution $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$ is realised by the entrywise Galois map $\sigma$ on the defining
      carrier.
      A bounded exact audit of the $120$-element icosian realisation of $2I$ (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}) shows that
      $\sigma$ does not preserve the carrier: it maps $2I$ onto its Galois-conjugate copy $\sigma(2I)\neq2I$, another
      binary icosahedral subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, while post-composition with $\sigma$ sends the defining
      character $\chi_2$ to the inequivalent character $\chi_{2'}$, with $\langle\chi_2\circ\sigma,\chi_2\rangle=0$.
      Hence the class of $\sigma$ is the nontrivial class of $\mathrm{Out}(2I)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ ($\sigma$ followed by a
      conjugation in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ carrying $\sigma(2I)$ back to $2I$ is an outer automorphism of $2I\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$),
      and $\sigma$ itself is not carrier-preserving.
      By contrast the Born--Infeld parity is the entrywise complex conjugation $\kappa$, which preserves the carrier
      and fixes both $\sqrt{5}$ and the golden ratio $\varphi$, so $\chi_2\circ\kappa=\chi_2$.
      An admissible operation, being carrier-preserving by [M], therefore cannot equal $\sigma$, and none implements
      $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{proposition}[Verticality of admissible fibre identifications, relative to {[M]}]
      \label{prop:verticality-admissible-identifications}
      Relative to [M], every fibre identification generated by the listed operations and the candidate Born--Infeld parity is vertical.
      It preserves the isomorphism class of each binary-icosahedral irreducible carrier and fixes the arithmetic type
      of the spinorial gate.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Relative to [M], the identification is generated by the candidate central pair $\{\chi,-\chi\}$ and
      operations of Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}: inner conjugation, the central element, the
      Born--Infeld parity, the Schur scalars, the projective involution $J_\Pi$, and the A4 locking (the Proposition
      lists the tensor functors and the Schur projection, which are carrier-preserving by Schur's lemma, in place of
      the Schur scalars here).
      This list contains the central element $-I$ of $2I$ and the parity $J_\Pi$, and does not name the central
      reflection $R_b$ separately, but $R_b$ belongs to the list that [M] makes complete (Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}),
      so under [M] the statement inherits clause (b), under [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, and clause (c) for the
      locking.
      Each acts inside the admissible carrier and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:no-horizontal-admissible-operation}, lies in the
      $\sqrt{5}$-fixing subgroup of $\Gal(K_q/\mathbb{Q})$.
      A fibre identification is a composition of such operations, hence is itself carrier-preserving and
      $\sqrt{5}$-fixing: it preserves the isomorphism class of every irreducible carrier and fixes
      $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, which is the arithmetic type of the spinorial gate.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Full-tower type preservation, relative to {[M]}]
      \label{cor:full-tower-type-preservation}
      Relative to [M], a finite tower of admissible recursive non-injective strata cannot generate the horizontal Galois
      exchange $2\leftrightarrow2'$.
      It can only compose vertical fibre identifications, hence it preserves the binary-icosahedral arithmetic type.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By Proposition~\ref{prop:verticality-admissible-identifications} each stratum identification is vertical.
      The $\sqrt{5}$-fixing operations form a subgroup, closed under composition, so any finite composition of vertical
      identifications is vertical and fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.
      The horizontal exchange $2\leftrightarrow2'$ is the outer automorphism, which moves $\sqrt{5}$ and is therefore
      not in this subgroup.
    \end{proof}

    The full-tower statement thus carries one status throughout this note: it holds relative to the modelling premise
    [M] (completeness of the list of Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}, which implies carrier-preservation under
    its clauses (a)--(d)), with [H-WS] for $R_b$ and the candidate base-fibre parity taken as a premise in the base-fibre paragraph of Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}; [M] is not
    proved.
    It does not assert that no external equivalence of abstract character tables exists; it shows that, relative to
    [M], the horizontal Galois exchange is not realised by an operation preserving the binary-icosahedral carrier.

  \section{The chiral lift: conditional consequences}
    \label{sec:chiral}

    The chiral orientation note~\cite{Beau2026cho} proves the orientation chain within the inherited Heisenberg
    central-phase convention and under [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, the chiral phase being the linear cyclotomic
    character $\omega_\chi(e)=\zeta_q^{\Delta A_c(e)}$, and leaves a single caveat: a possible additional spin-Galois
    phase of the full Lorentzian chiral lift, orthogonal to $\zeta_q$.
    This section applies Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity} to that caveat.
    We show that, under the inputs listed next, the caveat does not arise within the present spin stratum.

    \paragraph{Inputs of this section.}
    Five inputs enter, and none is derived here.
    (a)~[H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}, for the $R_b$ clause (parts~(i) and~(ii) of that hypothesis).
    (b)~The Lorentzian chiral lift: the identification of the $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ obtained by integrating
$\mathfrak{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ with the group acting on the chiral
split $S_L\oplus S_R$, together with the reading of $2I$ as a subgroup of $\mathrm{SU}(2)\subset\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$
acting on the doublet by its defining two-dimensional character (an identification, not contained in [H-Spin] and
used in Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl}).
    This note does not construct it.
    Its status is that of the spin-solder hypothesis [H-Spin] of Q14 (Hypothesis ``Lorentzian spin solder'' and
    Section~6 of~\cite{Beau2026q14}), whose part~(ii), the identification of this $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ with the
    spin group of the effective co-metric, Q14 states is supplied by no source, the metaplectic group itself not acting
    on the doublet.
    The chiral orientation note uses the $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ reading of the carrier with Q14 as its
    reference~\cite{Beau2026cho}.
    (c)~The modelling premise [M] of Section~\ref{sec:rigidity}.
    (d)~The inherited central-phase convention [C] of~\cite{Beau2026cho}: after the central-character convention and the
outgoing branch are fixed, the chiral landing phase is the linear character $\omega_\chi=\zeta_q^{\Delta A_c}$.
(e)~The boost premise [B], stated here and asserted, not derived: the lift extends $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}}$ only
by the central character and the complexification unit $\mathrm{i}$, the latter acting by $\mathrm{i}\mapsto-\mathrm{i}$
(complex conjugation); in particular it supplies no automorphism of $K_q(\mathrm{i})$ moving $\sqrt{5}$, although
$\Gal(K_q(\mathrm{i})/\mathbb{Q})$ contains such elements, so that [B] is a statement about the lift and not a
consequence of the field.
Lemma~\ref{lem:boost} and Corollary~\ref{cor:chiralrigidity} are stated under [H-Spin] (as input), [M], [B] and, for
the $R_b$ clause, [H-WS]; Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl} under [H-Spin] (as input); Corollary~\ref{cor:orient} inherits all of
them and adds [C].

    The object is the metaplectic lift of the ordered cascade step.
    The two cascade flows are the Weil images of the unipotent one-parameter subgroups of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$,
    with product $S_X(t)S_Y(s)=W\!\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1+ts&t\\s&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; a
    Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff reduction in $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ identifies the per-step $J_3$ component, to leading order, with the
    oriented product $ts$ of the two shear parameters, produced by the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutator $[E,F]=H$ in the
    $\mathrm{SL}(2)$-on-$V$ model, where no Heisenberg bracket is used~\cite{Beau2026prs,Beau2026aar}; no map between the two
    brackets is supplied.
    Under [H-Spin], the Lorentzian metric is read through $\mathfrak{mp}(2,\mathbb{R})_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb{C})$ and the
    chiral split $S_L\oplus S_R$~\cite{Beau2026q14}, and the chiral weight $\sigma_L$ classes $S_L$ versus $S_R$ by the Weyl
    representation type $\mathbf{2}$ versus $\bar{\mathbf{2}}$~\cite{Beau2026cho}.

    \begin{lemma}[The Lorentzian boost is Galois-internal, under {[H-Spin]} as input and the premise {[B]}]
      \label{lem:boost}
      Under [H-Spin] and the premise [B] of this section, the action induced on $K_q$ by the Lorentzian chiral lift extends
$\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{spin}}$ only by the central character and the analytic complexification unit $\mathrm{i}$, and
neither supplies an action on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$.
The central character contributes the cyclotomic phase $\zeta_q$, whose automorphisms form
$\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)/\mathbb{Q})$; the foundations propose the Born--Infeld parity, that is complex conjugation
$\zeta_q\mapsto\zeta_q^{-1}$, as the candidate fibre involution~\cite{Beau2026m} (a hypothesis there, taken here as a premise), and that no other element of this group
(and no element moving $\sqrt{5}$) is supplied is the premise [B], not a statement of the foundations.
      The metaplectic cocycle is absent in the inherited model~\cite{Beau2026cho}; a cocycle of Weil/Maslov type would be
      a further operation of the lift, and its exclusion is part of [B].
      The unit $\mathrm{i}$ is not in $K_q$ (for odd prime $q$ the quadratic subfields of $K_q$ are
      $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^{*}})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5q^{*}})$), so the boost is read in $K_q(\mathrm{i})$,
      where it enters by $\mathrm{i}\mapsto-\mathrm{i}$, that is by complex conjugation, which fixes $\sqrt{5}$; the group
      $\Gal(K_q(\mathrm{i})/\mathbb{Q})$ does contain elements moving $\sqrt{5}$, so the absence of such an action is the
premise [B], an assertion about what the lift supplies, not a consequence of the field.
Under [B] the boost lies in the $\sqrt{5}$-fixing subgroup: it is Galois-internal to the spin stratum.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{lemma}[Weyl conjugation versus Galois conjugation, under {[H-Spin]} as input]
      \label{lem:weyl}
      The chiral weight $\sigma_L$ classes the Weyl branches $S_L$ versus $S_R$ by complex-conjugation type,
      $\mathbf{2}$ versus $\bar{\mathbf{2}}$~\cite{Beau2026cho}, whereas the Galois action $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$
      classes the two two-dimensional irreducible characters $2$ versus $2'$ of $2I$, the swap realised by $\sigma$
      alone (Appendix~\ref{app:audit}).
      These distinctions are not the same.
      Under the restriction $2I\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)\subset\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ the representations of
      $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ are pseudoreal, so $S_L$ and $S_R$ restrict to the same two-dimensional carrier, while $2'$ is
      the Galois conjugate of that carrier and not the right Weyl branch.
      The spinorial grading $\chi(-I)/\chi(1)=-1$ that $\sigma_L$ reads is common to both two-dimensional irreducibles
      (Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}), and their $\sqrt{5}$ content sits only on the order-five and order-ten
      classes (Corollary~\ref{cor:tiebreak}), which the Weyl-conjugation label does not address.
      Hence $\sigma_L$ is orthogonal to the outer automorphism that distinguishes $2$ from $2'$.
    \end{lemma}

    \begin{corollary}[Spin-Galois type-rigidity of the Lorentzian chiral lift, under {[H-Spin]} as input, {[M]}, {[B]} and {[H-WS]}]
      \label{cor:chiralrigidity}
      Let $q$ be prime with $q\neq5$.
      Under [H-Spin] (taken as input, not derived here), the boost premise [B], relative to [M] and, for the $R_b$ clause of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}, under [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of},
      every field automorphism induced on $K_q$ by the Lorentzian chiral lift of the ordered cascade step acts
      trivially on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$; the nontrivial action, when present, is cyclotomic.
      In particular $\sigma_L$ lies in the parity factor $\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_q)/\mathbb{Q})$ and does not probe the
      Galois-spin factor $\Gal(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})/\mathbb{Q})$.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity} the spin-stratum operations fix $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, and by
      Lemma~\ref{lem:boost} (that is, under [B]) so does the Lorentzian boost; their generated group lies in the $\sqrt{5}$-fixing subgroup
      of $\Gal(K_q/\mathbb{Q})$, closed under composition and inverse.
      The only generator that could move $\sqrt{5}$ is the outer automorphism of $2I$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:weyl} is
      not coupled by $\sigma_L$ and is supplied by no operation of the lift.
      Hence the induced action on $K_q$ fixes $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, and any nontrivial part is cyclotomic.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{corollary}[Orientation within the present spin stratum, under {[H-Spin]} as input, {[H-WS]}, {[M]}, {[B]} and the inherited convention {[C]}]
      \label{cor:orient}
      Within the Lorentzian chiral lift generated by the present spin stratum, under [H-Spin] (as input), [H-WS], [M], [B] and
the inherited central-phase convention [C] of~\cite{Beau2026cho},
      \[
        \omega_\chi(e)=\zeta_q^{\Delta A_c(e)}
        \ \Longrightarrow\ \rho_\chi=1
        \ \Longrightarrow\ [\mathrm{H\text{-}orient}]
        \ \Longrightarrow\ N_A=\theta_{\max}\neq0 ,
      \]
      where $\rho_\chi=1$ holds by definition of $\sigma_L:=\operatorname{sgn}\Delta A_c$ on the nonzero-cocycle support
      (it is not a measured quantity), and the chain is that of~\cite{Beau2026cho,Beau2026q11of} within the inherited
      convention.
    \end{corollary}

    \begin{proof}
      By Corollary~\ref{cor:chiralrigidity} the chiral phase carries no Galois-spin factor orthogonal to $\zeta_q$, so
      the residual caveat left open by the chiral orientation note~\cite{Beau2026cho} does not arise.
      A cyclotomic reindexing of the phase by a quadratic Gauss (Weil/Maslov) sum is a cyclotomic quantity: for prime $q$
the sum $\sum_x\zeta_q^{cx^2}$ equals $\left(\tfrac{c}{q}\right)\sqrt{q^{*}}\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^{*}})$, and its
unit-modulus phase (divided by $\sqrt q$) lies in $\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})$ up to a sign, so the phase lies in
$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{4q})$, whose quadratic subfields are $\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})$, $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^{*}})$,
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q^{*}})$, none equal to $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ for $q\neq5$; it defines no spin-Galois phase.
This does not extend to Gauss sums of higher-order multiplicative characters: at the corpus primes
$q=61,101,151$ one has $5\mid q-1$, and sums of characters of order divisible by $5$ live in a field containing $\zeta_5$, hence $\sqrt{5}$ (for instance
$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5q})$).
Excluding them is part of the assumption [C] below.
      That such a reindexing is absorbed into the choice of the Heisenberg central block and its Born--Infeld
      conjugate, so that the landing phase stays the linear character $\omega_\chi(e)=\zeta_q^{\Delta A_c(e)}$ used in the
      inherited-lift calculation rather than an independent average over cyclotomic embeddings, is not argued here: it
is the assumption [C], the inherited convention of~\cite{Beau2026cho}, and it is also what keeps higher-order Gauss
sums out.
      The implications are then those of the inherited lift, now with no orthogonal factor available to set
      $\rho_\chi=0$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[Boundary with the Schur transport]

    The net statement is that $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ is a theorem modulo the ADE case-selection gate, and that, for the
    listed operations of the spin stratum and under the clauses above, no operation realises the Galois action $\sigma$:
    the generated group acts trivially on $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$, and the $\sqrt{5}$-fixing automorphisms form a subgroup, so
    no iteration, central lift, Schur projection or A4 locking built from these generators can induce $\sigma$.
    Whether the gate requires $\sigma$ is not established, so this note leaves the first-principles status of the gate
    unchanged: it remains open.
    Applied to the Lorentzian chiral lift, the same closure argument shows, under [H-Spin] (taken as input, not derived
here), [H-WS], [M], [B] and [C], that the lift induces no action on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$, so that the residual
    spin-Galois caveat of the chiral orientation note does not arise within the present spin stratum~\cite{Beau2026cho}.

    The downstream consequence bounds the relevance of the gate.
    The gate fixes a ratio of levels and hence the structural input $\epsilon$; it does not fix observable masses.
    The charged-lepton and quark spectra and the mixing matrices require the explicit construction of the operator
    $E_\Pi$ and the projected Yukawa sector, with the mass hierarchy carried by the cascade amplification and its
    exponent~\cite{Beau2026fmnote,Beau2026prs,Beau2026a4}.
    Even a derivation of the gate would not by itself produce a mass.
    The gate remains an open, characterisable structural sub-problem, which this note does not settle, while the
    quantitative effort proceeds on $E_\Pi$, the explicit Lorentzian block, and the Yukawa sector.

    \paragraph{Residual role of the ADE gate in the angular normalisation.}
    The Weil--BFS angular-capacity audit~\cite{Beau2026aar} fixes the first-shell (onset) coefficient $\tfrac13$ of the central-phase increment for the standard $\{\pm X,\pm Y\}$ campaign (angular under the $A_c$ input), but
    it does not derive the dictionary value $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$ from the $\mathrm{Sym}^2$ geometry alone.
    After removing the trivial angle factor $2\pi/q$, the reduced conversion problem is
    \[
      \frac{1}{3}\longmapsto \frac{1}{10},
      \qquad
      N_{A,\mathrm{red}}^{\mathrm{geom}}=\frac{3}{10}.
    \]
    The $\mathrm{Sym}^2(\mathbb{C}^2)$ carrier supplies the triplet dimension, the $J_3$-spectrum $\{0,\pm1\}$, the
    Hilbert--Schmidt norm $\langle J_3,J_3\rangle=2$, and the Born--Infeld $\sqrt2$ normalisation, but it does not fix
    the level ratio
    \[
      R=\frac{\tfrac12+\epsilon}{\tfrac12-\epsilon}.
    \]
    For $R=3/2$ one obtains $\epsilon=\tfrac{1}{10}$, equivalently $N_{A,\mathrm{red}}^{\mathrm{geom}}=\tfrac{3}{10}$.
    Thus the remaining angular-normalisation residue is not a fitted number: it is the level ratio $R$, which the choice of
    the generator class (the gate) supplies; this note does not relate that choice to $\sigma$.

    \paragraph{Concrete form of the remaining generator fork.}
    The residual ADE gate can be stated equivalently as a generator-set fork inside the binary icosahedral stratum.
    With the neutral admissibility convention, namely the spinorial order-four half-turn class, the class-complete
    spectrum has outer deficits
    \[
      \{|\lambda_1-1|,|\lambda_3-1|\}=\{\tfrac15,\tfrac13\},
    \]
    and therefore
    \[
      R_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}4}=\frac{1/3}{1/5}=\frac53.
    \]
    Inserted in the same deficit normal form as Lemma~\ref{lem:algebraic}, this would give
    \[
      \epsilon_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}4}=\frac{R_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}4}-1}{2(R_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}4}+1)}=\frac18,
      \qquad
      \kappa_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}4}=\frac{8}{15}.
    \]
    By contrast, the non-neutral order-five, or $\sqrt{5}$-locus, class has outer deficits
    \[
      \{|\lambda_1-1|,|\lambda_3-1|\}=\{\tfrac16,\tfrac14\},
    \]
    hence
    \[
      R_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}5}=\frac{1/4}{1/6}=\frac32,
      \qquad
      \epsilon_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}5}=\frac1{10},
      \qquad
      \kappa_{\mathrm{ord}\text{-}5}=\frac5{12}.
    \]
    Thus the issue is not the numerical origin of $3:2$ once the order-five class is selected: there it is a forced
    Cayley-spectral invariant.
    The issue is the selection of the order-five $\sqrt{5}$-locus rather than the neutral order-four locus.
    The latter is not a harmless convention change: it transports to the different value $\epsilon=\tfrac18$, not to
    $\epsilon=\tfrac1{10}$.

    The tempting fermionic shortcut, chirality selecting the locus, is not settled here either.
    The spinorial grading read by the Lorentzian chiral lift is common to the relevant two-dimensional spinorial types
    and, under [H-Spin], [H-WS], [M] and [B], induces no action on $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$~\cite{Beau2026cho}.
    Consequently, under these inputs and the statuses of~\cite{Beau2026cho}, chirality supplies the frontier orientation
    within the present spin stratum, but it does not supply $\sigma$.
    Since no link between the gate and $\sigma$ is established (Remark~\ref{rem:gate-link}), this note draws no conclusion
    on whether chirality selects the $\sqrt{5}$-locus: that datum remains the gate itself.

    \paragraph{Downstream mass-split gate.}
    The order-five Cayley-spectral ratio $R_{\mathrm{ADE}}=3/2$ induces the split value
    $\epsilon=(R_{\mathrm{ADE}}-1)/(2(R_{\mathrm{ADE}}+1))=1/10$.
    On the projected Yukawa side, the same formula rewrites the free diagonal parameter $u$ through
    $R_\Pi=(\tfrac12+u)/(\tfrac12-u)$, so that $\epsilon(R_\Pi)=u$~\cite{Beau2026pyo}.
    The remaining assertion $u=1/10$ is therefore precisely the dictionary identification $R_\Pi=R_{\mathrm{ADE}}$.
    It is not derived here: the results above bear on $\sigma$ only, not on this identification.

  \section{Reproducible audit of the spin-stratum operations}
    \label{app:audit}

    The audit is a finite computation, reproducible from the group with no hard-coded character data.
    The protocol is as follows.
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Construct $2I\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$ explicitly as the $120$ matrices over $\mathbb{F}_5$ of determinant one.
      \item Compute the conjugacy classes from the group: nine classes, of orders $1,2,3,4,5,5,6,10,10$ and sizes
      $1,1,20,30,12,12,20,12,12$.
      \item Reconstruct the character table from the class algebra by simultaneous diagonalisation of the class-sum
      matrices $A_i$, with $(A_i)_{jk}=a_{ikj}$ the class-multiplication constants, recovering the dimensions
      $1,2,2',3,3',4,4',5,6$.
      \item Validate by the row orthogonality relations, which hold to machine precision.
      The comparison of the reconstructed table with the published character table of $2I$~\cite{Beau2026a1} is by
      inspection and is not performed by the scripts.
      \item Apply the Galois action $\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$ to the table: it induces the permutation
      $(2\,2')(3\,3')$ of $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$, fixing $1,4,4',5,6$.
      On classes, the outer automorphism of $\mathrm{SL}(2,5)$ (conjugation by $\mathrm{diag}(2,1)$ in
      $\mathrm{GL}(2,5)$) exchanges $5a\leftrightarrow5b$ and $10a\leftrightarrow10b$ and fixes the classes of orders
      $1,2,3,4,6$; the order-four class is fixed and each of the loci $5a\cup5b$, $10a\cup10b$ is mapped onto itself.
      \item Compute the action of the operations covered by the audit: inner conjugations, complex conjugation
      $J_\Pi$ and the central element $-I$ induce the identity permutation of $\mathrm{Irr}(2I)$; none realises
      $2\leftrightarrow2'$.
      The central reflection $R_b=W(-I)$ is assigned the action of $-I$ under [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}; this is a
      hypothesis, not a computation.
      The tensor functors, the Schur projection and the A4 locking are not computed by the audit (clauses (c) and (d) of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}).
    \end{enumerate}

    The two two-dimensional irreducible characters on the $\sqrt{5}$-locus are Galois-conjugate, with
    $\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2$ and $\bar\varphi=\varphi-1=(\sqrt{5}-1)/2$:
    \[
      \begin{array}{c|cccc}
        & 5a & 5b & 10a & 10b\\
\hline
\chi_{2} & \bar\varphi & -\varphi & -\bar\varphi & \varphi\\
\chi_{2'} & -\varphi & \bar\varphi & \varphi & -\bar\varphi
      \end{array}
    \]
    where the labelling is fixed by the convention $10a:=-(5a)$, $10b:=-(5b)$ (so $\chi_2(-g)=-\chi_2(g)$ gives the
columns above, and the central lift exchanges $5a\leftrightarrow10a$, $5b\leftrightarrow10b$), and $\chi_2(5a)=\bar\varphi$
fixes the choice of $5a$ versus $5b$ (the table of~\cite{Beau2026a1} labels $10a$ with the value $\varphi$, that is $10a=-(5b)$ in this convention; the labels differ, the classes and values do not); so $\chi_{2'}=\sigma(\chi_2)$ with $\sigma:\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$, and the swap $2\leftrightarrow2'$ is realised
    by $\sigma$ alone.
    Since every character of $2I$ is real-valued, complex conjugation $J_\Pi$ fixes each irreducible representation; the
    central element $-I$ acts by the scalar $\chi(-I)/\chi(1)=\pm1$ without permuting, and so does $R_b=W(-I)$ under
    [H-WS] of~\cite{Beau2026q11of}; and inner
    conjugations are trivial on class functions.
    The $\sqrt{5}$-fixing automorphisms form a subgroup, so no composition of the operations above realises $\sigma$.
    This establishes clause (a) of Proposition~\ref{prop:spinrigidity}, and clause (b) under [H-WS].

    A complementary realisation audit supports the carrier-level statement of
    Lemma~\ref{lem:no-horizontal-admissible-operation}.
    Realising $2I$ as the $120$ icosians in $\mathrm{SU}(2)$, exact over $\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i},\sqrt{5})$, the
    entrywise Galois map $\sigma:\sqrt{5}\mapsto-\sqrt{5}$ does not preserve this set: it carries the icosians onto
    a different binary icosahedral subgroup $\sigma(2I)\neq 2I$, and $\chi_2\circ\sigma=\chi_{2'}$ with
    $\langle\chi_2\circ\sigma,\chi_2\rangle=0$.
    By contrast the entrywise complex conjugation $\kappa:\mathrm{i}\mapsto-\mathrm{i}$, the Born--Infeld parity,
    preserves $2I$ and fixes both $\sqrt{5}$ and the golden ratio $\varphi$, so $\chi_2\circ\kappa=\chi_2$.
    Thus the operation exchanging $2\leftrightarrow2'$ is not carrier-preserving, whereas every admissible operation is,
    by the premise [M] of Section~\ref{sec:rigidity} (with its clause statuses, in particular [H-WS] for $R_b$).
